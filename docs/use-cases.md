# Use Cases

ScanReady is designed for workflows where high-poly scans need to become lighter, cleaner and easier to use.

Raw 3D scans are often too heavy for realtime production. They may look detailed, but can be difficult to move, export, render or use in VR and game engines.

ScanReady helps convert those scans into optimized assets with baked textures, preserving the visual identity of the original capture.

---

## VR and AR

VR and AR experiences require stable performance.

A very dense scan can be too heavy for smooth interaction, especially on standalone headsets or realtime viewers.

ScanReady helps prepare scanned models for VR and AR by:

- reducing polygon count;
- creating a lighter mesh;
- baking visual detail into textures;
- supporting normal maps and ambient occlusion;
- helping make the asset easier to render;
- making the model more practical for interactive scenes.

This is useful for virtual museums, training simulations, cultural heritage experiences, product visualization and immersive environments.

---

## Video Games

Game engines require assets that balance visual quality and performance.

A raw scan with millions of polygons is rarely suitable for direct use in a video game.

ScanReady helps prepare game-ready assets by:

- creating an optimized low-poly version;
- generating UVs;
- preparing a baking cage;
- baking texture information from the high-poly scan;
- saving texture maps for external workflows;
- keeping detail in textures instead of heavy geometry.

This makes scanned props, environment elements and real objects easier to bring into a game pipeline.

---

## Realtime Visualization

Realtime visualization requires assets that can be inspected, moved and rendered smoothly.

ScanReady is useful when displaying scanned objects in:

- interactive product viewers;
- architectural previews;
- web 3D viewers;
- realtime Blender scenes;
- realtime rendering tools;
- lightweight review scenes.

The add-on helps reduce scene weight while keeping the object visually recognizable.

---

## Cultural Heritage

Cultural heritage scans often contain valuable surface detail, but the raw data can be very heavy.

ScanReady can help create lighter versions for:

- virtual exhibitions;
- museum previews;
- educational experiences;
- online collections;
- interactive archives;
- VR historical reconstructions.

The goal is not to replace the original scan archive.

The goal is to create a practical version for presentation, interaction and realtime exploration.

---

## Photogrammetry Assets

Photogrammetry often produces detailed but dense meshes.

ScanReady helps process photogrammetry models by:

- simplifying captured geometry;
- creating usable UVs;
- baking visual information from the source scan;
- saving texture maps;
- preparing assets for further editing or export.

This can be useful for rocks, statues, props, terrain sections, objects, ruins, artifacts and captured real-world surfaces.

---

## Cleaning Up Large Scans

Large scans can slow down Blender and make editing difficult.

ScanReady helps create a more manageable asset that is easier to:

- orbit in the viewport;
- inspect;
- bake;
- export;
- use in scenes;
- share with collaborators.

The optimized result can be used as a lighter production asset while keeping the original high-poly scan as the source.

---

## Typical Workflow

A typical ScanReady use case is:

1. Import or open a high-poly scan in Blender.
2. Select the scan object.
3. Use **ONE CLICK BAKE** for a quick automatic result.
4. Check the optimized mesh and baked textures.
5. Use the manual steps if the asset requires more control.
6. Export the final asset for VR, video games, realtime visualization or presentation.

---

## Main Benefit

ScanReady helps bridge the gap between scan quality and realtime usability.

It turns heavy captured models into assets that are easier to manage, bake and use in production.

The result is a lighter version of the scan. Quality and performance should be checked in the target scene and on the target device, considering geometry, textures and materials together.
